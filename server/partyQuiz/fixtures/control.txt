Sourdough starts with flour and water left in a warm kitchen for a few days. Wild yeast from the
flour and the air begins to feed, and small bubbles appear on the surface of the jar each morning.

Feed the starter twice a day with equal weights of flour and water. After a week it should double
in size within six hours of a feed and smell pleasantly sour, a little like yoghurt or green apples.

To bake, mix the starter with more flour, water and salt, then fold the dough every half hour for
two hours. Shape it into a round, let it rise slowly overnight in a cool place, and bake it hot.

A heavy pot with a lid traps steam during the first twenty minutes, which gives a thin crisp crust.
Let the loaf cool for an hour before cutting it, or the crumb will be gummy in the middle.
